# RELATE DATING BLUEPRINT
## Technical and Clinical Specification
### Section 6: The Report Architecture and Writing Standard

---

## The Report as the Product

The questions are the instrument. The scoring engine is the infrastructure. The report is the product. Everything built before this section exists to make the report possible. The quality of the report is therefore not a writing consideration that follows the technical build. It is a technical requirement that governs the entire architecture. Every decision documented in the preceding sections, the indirect inference principle, the experiential question format, the dynamic Quadrant Four construction, the emergent pattern library, the synthesis architecture, was made in service of producing a report that meets the standard described in this section.

That standard is the quality of the best professional deep personality reports available. Not their length. Not their specific constructs. Their quality of knowing. The best instruments in this category produce reports that feel, to the person reading them, like exposure rather than description. They make claims the person did not volunteer. They name patterns the person has felt but never had language for. They describe the experience of being that person from the inside with enough precision that the recognition response arrives before the evaluative response, before the person has had time to decide whether to accept or reject the claim. That sequence, recognition before evaluation, is what the Blueprint report must produce consistently.

This section specifies exactly how to produce it.

---

## Report Structure

The individual report contains six sections in the following order. Section length targets are minimums and maximums enforced by the report generation engine through prompt engineering rather than word counting. The engine is instructed to write to the depth the content requires within the specified range.

**Section One: Relational History**
400 to 500 words. Builds from the Quadrant One Profile. Covers the character of the formative wound, what the nervous system concluded, whether that conclusion was revised, and what the unrevised or partially revised conclusion is still doing in present relationships. Does not use clinical language. Does not reference the scoring. Speaks entirely in the language of experience.

**Section Two: The Emotion Underneath**
400 to 500 words. Builds from the Quadrant Two Profile. Names the trigger emotion directly and immediately without hedging. Describes the phenomenology of the trigger from the inside, what arrives first, what it feels like in the body, what it produces in behavior, and what it costs in the specific relational contexts where it is most active. Draws the first cross-Quadrant connection to the Quadrant One Profile, explaining why this emotion makes complete sense given this history.

**Section Three: How You Navigate Uncertainty**
400 to 500 words. Builds from the Quadrant Three Profile. Describes the decision mode from the inside of the experience rather than from a behavioral description. Names what the mode feels like when it is running, what it produces in the relationship, and what partners experience on the receiving end of it. Draws the cross-Quadrant connection to the Quadrant Two trigger emotion, naming the specific sequence that the emotion running through this decision mode produces.

**Section Four: Your Persona in This Context**
450 to 550 words. Builds from the Quadrant Four Profile using the persona code, the three axis scores, and the persona metadata as inputs. Acknowledges the persona as a genuine expression of character before examining where it is doing defensive work. Names the specific amplification pattern under romantic pressure. Draws the cross-Quadrant connection to all three preceding sections, describing how the history shaped the persona, what the persona is managing on behalf of the trigger emotion, and where the decision mode and the persona interact under maximum load.

**Section Five: The Portrait**
500 to 600 words. The synthesis section. Built entirely from cross-Quadrant inference as specified in Section 5 of this document. Does not repeat material from the preceding four sections. Performs the four synthesis moves in sequence: the through-line, the primary cross-Quadrant claim, the genuine strengths of this configuration, and the specific difficulty named with precision. If an emergent pattern applies, uses the named pattern frame as the organizing architecture for this section.

**Section Six: What This Means for Partnership**
300 to 400 words. The partner-facing section. Addressed to a current or future partner rather than to the user. Written in a warmer, more practical register than the preceding sections. Tells the partner what they are actually dealing with, what the behavior is protecting, what helps and what makes it worse, and what this person needs that they will probably not ask for directly. The shift in addressee is marked clearly at the opening of this section so the user understands they are now reading something written for someone else.

**Section Seven: The Growing Edge**
200 to 300 words. Addressed back to the user. Closes the report. Specifies two or three concrete, experiential invitations rather than behavioral prescriptions. Does not instruct the user to change. Invites them toward something specific that their particular configuration makes them capable of but has not yet made easy. Written as genuine invitation rather than clinical recommendation. The tone is warm and direct simultaneously.

## The Writing Standard

The following principles are technical requirements for the report generation engine. They are not stylistic preferences. They are the specific writing behaviors that produce deep personality report quality. Each principle is stated, explained, and followed by an example of a compliant sentence and a non-compliant sentence covering the same content. The non-compliant examples are included because the failure modes are as important to understand as the correct approach.

**Principle One: Confident Non-Obvious Inference**

The report says things the person did not tell it. These are not guesses. They are inferences derived from the scoring with enough clinical grounding that they land as recognition rather than speculation. The report does not describe what the scoring found. It makes claims about the person's interior experience that the scoring made possible.

The word non-obvious is the operative word. An obvious inference from a fear of abandonment trigger is that the person fears being left. That is the label, not the insight. A non-obvious inference from the same trigger is that the person has developed a specific radar for the early signals of withdrawal that runs faster than their conscious attention, so that they are often responding to a threat that their partner has not yet consciously registered, which makes the response look disproportionate from the outside even when it is internally calibrated to something real.

Compliant: You have developed a sensitivity to the early grammar of withdrawal that most people cannot read. You notice the change in cadence before the change in content, the slight delay before the response, the quality of presence that is present and also elsewhere. You are often right. You are also often responding to something that has not yet happened, which means your partner is frequently defending against an accusation they do not understand.

Non-compliant: You may sometimes feel anxious when a partner seems distant. People with your Profile often struggle with uncertainty in relationships and may need reassurance from partners.

**Principle Two: The Experience Before the Interpretation**

The report earns every inference by demonstrating it already understands what it feels like to be this person before it names what is underneath. The interpretation does not lead. The experience leads. The interpretation follows from the experience and feels like a conclusion the reader has arrived at alongside the report rather than a verdict delivered from outside.

This principle governs the structure of every paragraph that makes a significant claim. The claim is always preceded by a description of the experience that makes the claim feel inevitable rather than imposed.

Compliant: There is a specific kind of quiet that descends when a relationship reaches a certain depth and you have not yet found a way out of it. It is not unhappiness. It is more like the feeling of being in a room that has gotten smaller without anyone moving. The analysis intensifies. The frameworks multiply. You are thinking about the relationship more than you are in it, and some part of you knows this, and the knowing does not change it. This is not avoidance in the ordinary sense. It is what happens when connection becomes threat and the only tool available is a mind that is very good at producing the appearance of engagement while maintaining a careful distance from the actual experience of being known.

Non-compliant: Your intellectualization coping style means you tend to analyze relationships rather than engage with them emotionally. This is a defense mechanism that protects you from vulnerability.

**Principle Three: No Hedging**

The report does not use hedging language under any circumstance. The specific words and phrases listed below are forbidden in the report narrative. They are permitted only in the user-facing explanatory material outside the report itself, such as onboarding instructions or FAQ content.

Forbidden: may, might, sometimes, often, tend to, can, could, for many people, people with your Profile, some people find, it is possible that, you might notice, this may not apply to everyone, in certain situations, depending on the context.

The hedging prohibition applies to the entire report with one exception. The growing edge section may use language of invitation and possibility because it is describing what the person is moving toward rather than what is already true. Even in this section, hedging about the present is forbidden. Only the future can be held with appropriate tentativeness.

Compliant: You make decisions about people quickly and you trust those decisions completely. The speed is intelligence. It is also a mechanism for settling ambiguity before it can be tolerated, which means you are sometimes certain about someone before you have actually met them.

Non-compliant: You may sometimes find that you tend to make quick judgments about people, which could be a sign that you might be uncomfortable with uncertainty in some situations.

**Principle Four: Mechanistic Bullets**

When the report uses bullet points, each bullet is a complete mechanistic sentence that describes a specific internal process or behavioral pattern with enough clinical precision that the reader recognizes the mechanism rather than just the label. Bullets are not used for lists of traits, characteristics, or adjectives. They are used only for sequences, patterns, or behavioral dynamics that benefit from visual separation.

The test for a compliant bullet is whether it could stand alone as a sentence in a clinical case note written by a therapist who understands this person well. If it reads like a label or a trait descriptor, it fails the test.

Compliant bullet: You move toward people with a quality of attention that makes them feel, correctly, that they are the only thing in the room, and you sustain that attention past the point where most people would have redirected it, which creates an intimacy that is real and also functions as a way of keeping the focus off you.

Non-compliant bullet: Strong empathy and attunement to others.

**Principle Five: Shadow Named Without Pathology**

Every section that names a cost, a limitation, a defensive pattern, or a shadow quality of the Profile does so without clinical pathologizing, without moral judgment, and without framing the pattern as a defect. The framing is always: this developed for a reason, the reason made sense, and the pattern is now costing something it does not have to cost.

This framing is not a softening of the truth. It is a more accurate account of how defensive patterns develop than the defect model provides. The report is honest about costs precisely because it contextualizes them rather than labeling them. A report that says you are avoidant is less honest than a report that says you learned to hold people at the specific distance that kept you safe in conditions where closeness was unpredictable, and that distance is now a default that runs even when closeness is no longer dangerous.

Compliant: The care you extend to people who need you is genuine. It is also the one form of connection that does not require you to be uncertain, because when you are helping, you know where you stand. What you have not yet fully explored is what happens to connection when neither person needs anything. That territory, where love is not a function but a simple fact, is the one you have the least practice navigating.

Non-compliant: Your caretaking behavior is a defense mechanism that prevents genuine intimacy. You use helping others to avoid vulnerability.

**Principle Six: Second Person Throughout**

The report speaks directly to the person in the second person at all times. It does not refer to people with your Profile or those who score in this range or individuals in this category. It speaks to you, this person, about this person's specific experience. The second person is maintained even when the report is drawing on general clinical knowledge about a Profile, because the general clinical knowledge is always translated into specific experiential language before it appears in the report.

The shift to third person in any section of the report, even momentarily, breaks the quality of direct address that makes the report feel like knowing rather than categorizing. There are no exceptions to this principle in the first six sections. The partner-facing section in Section Six shifts to a different second person, addressing the partner rather than the user, and this shift is marked explicitly.

**Principle Seven: No Clinical Jargon in the Narrative**

The report does not use the following terms in the narrative sections: attachment style, trigger, defense mechanism, nervous system, coping strategy, schema, integration level, profile, dimension, axis, scoring, avoidant, anxious, disorganized, secure, hyperactivation, deactivation, or any other term drawn from attachment theory, schema therapy, psychodynamic theory, or behavioral psychology that the average person would recognize as clinical language.

The concepts these terms represent are present in the report. The terms are not. The concepts are translated into experiential and descriptive language that conveys the same precision without positioning the reader as a subject being analyzed. This translation is not a simplification. It is a higher standard of writing. Saying you learned to leave before you could be left requires more craft than saying you exhibit preemptive avoidance as a defense against abandonment anxiety. It also produces recognition rather than assessment.

**Principle Eight: Earned Intimacy**

The report's tone becomes progressively more direct and intimate as it moves through the six sections. The Relational History section is the most measured in tone, because it is covering the most sensitive territory and the trust between the report and the reader has not yet been established. By the Portrait section, the report has earned the right to its most direct claims because it has demonstrated across four sections that it understands this person's experience from the inside. The growing edge section is the most intimate in tone because it is making an invitation, which requires the warmest register the report uses.

This progression is intentional and must be maintained. A report that opens at maximum intimacy and directness has not earned that register. A report that remains measured throughout has not used the space the earlier sections created. The progression from measured to direct to intimate is the emotional arc of the report and it mirrors the experience of being genuinely known by another person, which begins with being seen accurately and arrives at being addressed directly.

## Forbidden Moves

The following are specific writing behaviors that appear in lower-quality psychological reporting and are prohibited in the Blueprint report without exception.

Using more than one direct question in any single section. Questions are powerful tools in psychological narrative because they invite the reader into reflection. Used more than once per section they become a rhetorical habit that replaces the declarative confidence the report requires.

Opening any paragraph with a generalization before making a specific claim. The specific claim leads. The context follows if it is needed. Never the reverse.

Using the word journey in any context. It is the most overused word in the wellness and self-development industry and signals immediately that the report is operating in that register rather than the clinical depth register the Blueprint requires.

Using the phrase this does not mean followed by a softer restatement of a difficult claim. The difficult claim stands on its own. Softening it after delivering it signals ambivalence about the claim and teaches the reader to discount what precedes the softening.

Ending any section with a general affirmation, a note of hope, or a reminder that growth is possible. These closings are characteristic of wellness content and are not characteristic of writing that takes the reader seriously. Each section ends when it has finished saying what it needed to say. The growing edge section carries the forward-looking energy for the entire report. The other sections do not need to share that weight.

Listing strengths and challenges as parallel bullet lists. This format implies that the strengths and challenges are separate qualities rather than two expressions of the same underlying system. The Portrait section in particular must resist this structure. The strength and the shadow are always named in relation to each other, because they are the same thing.

## Inputs the Report Generation Engine Receives

For each individual report generation call, the engine receives the following structured input object. Every field is required. The engine must not generate a report with missing fields. If a field is missing, the call fails and returns an error to the calling function rather than generating a partial report.

The user's attachment type from the RELATE assessment, expressed as the type name rather than a code.

The user's RELATE persona code and persona name.

The persona metadata fields from relate_persona_definitions.js for this persona code: the traits description, the datingBehavior array, the inRelationships array, the struggles array, the disappointments array, and the howValued array.

The Quadrant One Profile identifier and the three Dimension scores that produced it.

The Quadrant Two Profile identifier, which is the trigger emotion name.

The Quadrant Three Profile identifier and the two internal axis scores.

The Quadrant Four axis scores for defense, awareness, and amplification, and the composite Profile descriptor derived from them.

The emergent pattern identifier if one was assigned by the scoring engine, or null if no named pattern applies.

The confidence scores for each Quadrant Profile assignment.

Any narrative response content from the question engine that the scoring engine flagged as high-value experiential material for the report. This content is passed as a separate array of flagged response fragments that the report generation engine may draw on to make the narrative feel grounded in this specific person's stated experience rather than in the Profile description alone. The engine uses this material as texture and reference rather than quoting it directly.

## System Prompt Architecture

The report generation engine is called once per report section rather than once for the full report. This architecture produces higher quality output than a single full-report generation call because it allows each section's prompt to be built with the specific inputs and instructions relevant to that section rather than requiring a single prompt to hold all instructions simultaneously.

Each section prompt contains: the universal writing standard instructions from this section of the specification, the section-specific instructions including the target length and the cross-Quadrant connections to draw, the relevant subset of the input object for this section, the flagged experiential material relevant to this section's territory, and the output from any preceding sections passed as context so the section can build on rather than repeat what came before.

The synthesis section prompt receives the outputs of all four preceding sections as context and is explicitly instructed not to repeat their content. It receives the emergent pattern frame if one applies and the full set of cross-Quadrant interaction logic relevant to this specific four-Quadrant combination as specified in Section 5.

The partner-facing section prompt receives the full individual report as context and is instructed to shift addressee and register while drawing on the portrait the report has built rather than introducing new clinical claims.

The growing edge section prompt receives the full individual report as context and is instructed to derive the specific invitations from what the report has established about this person's configuration rather than generating generic growth recommendations.

## Quality Standard for Generated Output

Before any report section is returned to the user, it is evaluated against the following minimum quality criteria. Sections that fail any criterion are regenerated with a refined prompt rather than returned.

The section makes at least two non-obvious inferences that go beyond what the Profile description alone would produce.

The section uses no hedging language as defined in the forbidden list in Principle Three.

The section does not use clinical jargon as defined in Principle Seven.

The section maintains second-person address throughout, with the exception of the partner-facing section.

The section does not open with a generalization.

The section does not close with a general affirmation.

The section falls within the specified word count range for its position in the report.

The section draws the cross-Quadrant connection specified for its position.

These criteria are implemented as a structured evaluation prompt that assesses the generated section before it is returned. The evaluation prompt returns a pass or fail determination for each criterion with a brief explanation of any failures. Failed sections are regenerated with the failure explanation appended to the original prompt as a correction instruction.
