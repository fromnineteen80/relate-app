# RELATE DATING BLUEPRINT
## Technical and Clinical Specification
### Section 1: Product Overview

---

## What the Blueprint Is

The Dating Blueprint is a supplemental deep personality instrument that sits on top of the existing RELATE assessment. It does not replace the RELATE assessment. It extends it into territory the assessment was not designed to reach.

The RELATE assessment tells someone who they are as a dater. It produces a four-dimensional persona, an attachment style result, and a market positioning framework. These outputs are accurate and useful. What they do not produce is a portrait of how this specific person loves at the level of mechanism. They describe the pattern. They do not explain why the pattern exists, what emotion is running it underneath the behavior, what happens in the critical gap between feeling and action, or where the persona the person has built is serving them versus quietly costing them.

The Blueprint answers those questions. It does this through four quadrants, each measuring a distinct and clinically grounded dimension of relational psychology. The four quadrants interact with each other and with the existing RELATE outputs to produce a report that operates at a level of specificity and depth that no attachment type label or persona description can reach on its own.

The quality standard the Blueprint targets is the best professional Enneagram reporting available, the instruments that produce 40 pages of narrative that feel, to the person reading them, less like a description and more like exposure. Those reports make claims the person did not volunteer. They name patterns the person has felt but never had language for. They describe the experience of being that person from the inside with enough precision that the recognition response is immediate and the discomfort that follows it is productive rather than defensive. That is the standard the Blueprint report is built to meet.

---

## What the Blueprint Is Not

The Blueprint is not a clinical instrument. It does not diagnose, assess pathology, or produce results that should be used in therapeutic or medical contexts. It is a psychoeducational framework built on established clinical constructs, designed to give people language for patterns they already live but may not yet understand.

This positioning is deliberate and important. The best professional Enneagram instruments do not claim to be clinical tools either. They claim to be rigorous, research-grounded frameworks for self-understanding. The Blueprint makes the same claim. The clinical depth of the constructs is what produces the insight quality. The psychoeducational framing is what keeps the product honest about what it is and what it is not.

This distinction also matters legally and ethically. The report does not tell people what is wrong with them. It tells them how they work. Those are different things, and the language of the report must reflect that difference at every point.

## How the Blueprint Sits Inside the RELATE Ecosystem

The user arrives at the Blueprint having already completed the RELATE assessment. Two outputs from that assessment feed directly into the Blueprint as fixed inputs that the Blueprint does not re-measure.

The first input is the attachment style result. The Blueprint receives the user's attachment type as a known coordinate. It does not ask questions designed to re-derive it. Instead it uses the attachment type as the frame inside which all four quadrant results are interpreted. The quadrant results explain the attachment type at a level of mechanism and specificity that the type label alone cannot provide.

The second input is the persona code. The user's four-letter RELATE persona code, along with the behavioral metadata associated with that persona, is passed into the Blueprint's fourth quadrant. Quadrant Four does not re-measure the persona dimensions. It takes the persona as established and measures three things the persona result does not tell you: how rigidly the person is identified with this persona in dating contexts specifically, whether the persona is being expressed as genuine character or deployed as a defense system, and where romantic activation amplifies the persona beyond its healthy range.

The Blueprint also receives, as context, any other outputs the existing assessment has produced that are relevant to quadrant scoring or report generation. The checkpoint system that manages the existing assessment's session architecture will need to be extended to include a Blueprint session with its own checkpoint configuration.

---

## Two Modes: Single and Couples

The Blueprint operates in two distinct modes that share the same question engine and scoring architecture but produce different report outputs.

In single mode, the user completes the Blueprint independently. They receive an individual report organized around the four quadrant results plus a synthesis section that draws cross-quadrant inferences no individual quadrant section could produce alone. The individual report also includes a partner-facing section written in a different register than the self-facing sections. The partner-facing section is addressed to a future or current partner and tells them what they are actually dealing with when they are in relationship with this person, what the behavior is protecting, and what this person needs that they will probably not ask for directly.

In couples mode, both partners complete the Blueprint independently using separate accounts. Each receives their individual report first. Then a couples overlay report is generated that uses both sets of quadrant results to describe the specific dynamic the two people produce together. The couples overlay is not a compatibility score. It is a portrait of the particular relational system these two people have built, with specific attention to where their quadrant combinations produce the patterns they keep having, what the recurring argument is actually about underneath its surface content, and what both people need to understand about their own contribution before the dynamic can change.

The couples overlay requires that both partners have completed both the RELATE assessment and the Blueprint. Partner linking uses the existing account architecture. The overlay report is generated fresh from both complete result sets and is accessible to both partners simultaneously.

## The User Experience Arc

The Blueprint is positioned as a deepening, not a correction. The user has already received their RELATE results. They understand their persona and their attachment type at the level those results provide. The Blueprint begins from that understanding and takes them somewhere those results cannot go.

The question experience should feel different from the RELATE assessment. Where the RELATE assessment asks behavioral and preference questions, the Blueprint asks experiential and narrative questions. The user is not reporting what they generally do. They are being asked to remember specific moments, describe what happened in their body, and engage with the felt texture of their own relational history. This is a different cognitive and emotional register, and the question design must honor that difference consistently.

The report experience should feel like being known. Not assessed. Not categorized. Known. The person reading it should encounter sentences that describe their interior experience with enough precision that the recognition response arrives before they have had time to evaluate the claim. That recognition is the product. Everything else is the mechanism for producing it.
