## Pricing Decision

The existing pricing ladder runs from Free at zero through Plus at $29.99, Premium at $49.99, Pro at $69.99, and Couples at $119. The Couples report is the ceiling and is framed as the most comprehensive product.

The Attachment Style is priced as follows.

**Individual Attachment Style: $49 one-time**
Available to any user who has completed the RELATE assessment regardless of current tier. It is not a tier upgrade. It does not replace any existing tier. Users on the free tier must purchase any paid tier before accessing the Attachment Style, since the Attachment Style deepens the existing results and requires a complete scored profile to function.

**Couples Attachment Style: $79 one-time**
Both partners receive the full individual Attachment Style plus the couples overlay report, the trigger emotion collision analysis, the decision architecture gap portrait, and the shared couples growth track. This is priced at a deliberate discount from two individual purchases at $49 each to incentivize the use case that produces the richest product experience.

The rationale for $49 individual pricing:

It sits between Plus ($29.99) and Premium ($49.99) in price but delivers a categorically different kind of output than either, signaling that this is a different instrument rather than an incremental upgrade. It preserves the Couples report at $119 as the undisputed ceiling of the ladder. The couples Attachment Style at $79 sits comfortably below the Couples report, reinforcing the existing product hierarchy while creating a natural ladder within the Attachment Style product itself. A user who has purchased the individual Attachment Style and later purchases the Couples report should receive a $20 credit toward the couples Attachment Style, recognizing their prior investment.

Dating Attachment Style Methodology

**Opening paragraph:**
The Dating Attachment Style extends the RELATE assessment into territory that persona typing and conflict profiling cannot reach on their own. Where the five assessment modules identify behavioral patterns, the Attachment Style measures what is producing them. It does so through four Quadrants, each grounded in a distinct body of clinical research, that together map the psychological architecture underneath the patterns the main assessment found.

---

**H3: The Four Quadrants**

**Relational History**

The first Quadrant measures the formative wound architecture: not what happened to the person but what their nervous system concluded about love as a result, and whether that conclusion was ever revised.

This Quadrant is grounded in research on earned versus unearned security from the Adult Attachment Interview tradition developed by Mary Main and colleagues at Berkeley. The AAI established that the resolution status of early attachment disruptions is more predictive of adult relational behavior than the disruptions themselves. A significant rupture followed by genuine repair produces a different nervous system than the same rupture left unresolved, even when the surface behavioral presentation looks identical.

The Quadrant measures three dimensions: whether the primary disruption was chronic and ambient or acute and event-based, whether it originated with a caregiver or a romantic partner, and whether it was repaired, partially repaired, or remains unresolved. The combination of these three dimensions produces one of six named Profiles that the report uses as the foundation for explaining where the user's patterns came from.

**Trigger Emotion**

The second Quadrant measures the primary emotional driver underneath attachment behavior when relational threat activates. This is distinct from the emotional drivers measured in Module 4, which identifies the four conflict-specific drivers of abandonment, engulfment, inadequacy, and injustice. The Trigger Emotion Quadrant measures the deeper emotional frequency the nervous system defaults to under threat, which shapes not just conflict behavior but all attachment behavior across the relationship.

The five Trigger Emotion Profiles draw on affect theory in the tradition of Silvan Tomkins, shame research developed by Gershen Kaufman and extended by Brené Brown, Gottman's research establishing contempt as the single strongest predictor of relational deterioration, trauma-informed models of grief as an organizing relational state, and the distinction between rage as aggression and rage as a sovereignty response developed within contemporary trauma theory.

The same attachment type produces completely different relational behavior depending on which trigger emotion is underneath it. An anxious attachment style driven by shame pursues differently, conflicts differently, and needs different things from a repair conversation than an anxious attachment style driven by fear of abandonment. The Trigger Emotion Quadrant provides the differentiation that attachment type classification alone cannot.

**Decision Architecture**

The third Quadrant measures what happens in the gap between feeling something and acting on it under relational ambiguity. This is the territory that determines most of what partners experience, because the behavior that follows from the decision mode is what is visible from the outside, not the emotion that preceded it.

The six Decision Mode Profiles draw on Gottman's research on emotional flooding and the physiological recovery window during which rational decision-making is compromised, attachment research on hyperactivating and deactivating regulatory strategies developed by Phillip Shaver and Mario Mikulincer, and cognitive behavioral research on rumination, catastrophizing, and backward anchoring as specific patterns that operate during relational uncertainty.

The Quadrant scores the user on two internal dimensions: whether the default mode moves toward or away from the threat, and whether it operates primarily in the cognitive, behavioral, or relational register. These dimensions produce a dominant Profile and a secondary mode that the report uses to describe both the user's behavior and what partners experience on the receiving end of it.

**Persona in Practice**

The fourth Quadrant measures how the user's RELATE persona is currently operating in dating and relationship contexts specifically. It does not re-measure the persona. It takes the persona code established in Module 2 as a fixed input and measures three things the main assessment cannot reach from the outside: how rigidly the persona is currently identified with in romantic contexts, how aware the user is of its defensive register, and where romantic activation amplifies the persona beyond its healthy expression.

This Quadrant is grounded in schema therapy's distinction between coping modes and core needs developed by Jeffrey Young, Internal Family Systems research on protective parts that develop as adaptations to specific relational conditions, and the ego syntonic versus ego dystonic distinction from psychodynamic theory applied to relational identity.

The question set for this Quadrant is constructed dynamically at session initialization using the user's persona code to retrieve the relevant behavioral metadata from the RELATE persona library. The questions probe the shadow expressions already identified in each persona's profile, the moments where partners have historically named the cost of the persona, and the specific amplification patterns that appear when the relationship becomes high-stakes. The Quadrant Four question set is different for each of the 32 personas while the scoring architecture remains universal across all of them.

---

**H3: The Report**

The Attachment Style produces a 3,000-word narrative report organized across six sections. The report is generated through the Anthropic Claude API using a section-by-section prompt architecture that builds context progressively rather than generating the full report in a single call. Each section receives the outputs of preceding sections as context, which allows the report to develop a coherent portrait across its full length rather than producing six independent section summaries.

The report is written in the second person throughout and addresses the user directly at all times. It does not use clinical terminology in the narrative. It makes confident, specific inferences from the scoring that go beyond what the Profile descriptions alone would produce, operating in the register of a clinician who understands this person's specific combination of results rather than describing a general type. The writing standard targets the depth, specificity, and recognition quality of the best professional personality instruments available.

Each section is evaluated against a structured quality rubric before it is returned. Sections that do not meet the minimum criteria for non-obvious inference, direct address, and absence of hedging language are regenerated before the user receives them.

---

**H3: The Growth System**

The Attachment Style generates a personalized growth plan alongside the report. The growth plan connects the Attachment Style findings back to the full RELATE profile, producing twelve to fifteen journaling prompts derived from the user's specific Quadrant combination, two to three behavioral experiments precise enough to be actionable, and an early warning indicator set that tells the user what to watch for as the patterns identified in the Attachment Style activate in current or developing relationships.

The Attachment Style also introduces a gamified development track that extends the existing RELATE gamification system. The track identifies the one or two RELATE persona dimensions most implicated by the user's Attachment Style findings and focuses the development work there. As users engage with the growth plan material over time, the system tracks self-reported evidence of movement along those dimensions. Users who demonstrate sustained engagement are invited to retake the relevant assessment modules. A new persona code reached through this pathway represents genuine development rather than algorithmic reclassification. The user's full persona history is preserved in their profile, and the distance between the original code and the new one is the record of their growth.

For couples, the Attachment Style generates a third document: a couples overlay that uses both partners' Quadrant results to name the specific dynamic their combination produces together. The overlay draws on a complete trigger emotion collision matrix covering all ten possible pairings of the five trigger emotions and a decision architecture gap library covering the most clinically significant mode collisions. The couples overlay names what each person is experiencing in the moment the recurring pattern activates, from inside their own experience simultaneously, and tells both people what they each need to understand about their own contribution before the pattern can shift.
